01 · About

Researcher

My academic background lies at the intersection of Quantitative Sociology, Public Health and research methods. My work focuses on understanding social life through innovative quantitative methods, with a particular interest in time-use research.

I completed an ESRC 1+3 fully funded doctoral studentship at the University of Warwick, comprising an integrated Master's in Social Science Research Methods and a PhD in Sociology. During my doctoral studies, I received several awards, including an ESRC Overseas Institutional Visit award and an ESRC academic events award to co-organise the Quantitative Methods Doctoral Conference at Warwick.

Following my doctorate, I was awarded a part-time postdoctoral fellowship at the Institute of Advanced Study, University of Warwick. This provided opportunities for interdisciplinary collaboration, innovative dissemination and cross-disciplinary research, alongside my continuing work at the Centre for Time Use Research (CTUR), UCL.

02 · Research

Research &
Networks

My research combines methodological development with substantive questions in sociology and public health. I am particularly interested in time-use diary data and in developing methods that enable researchers to better understand how people allocate their time.

Time-use research
At CTUR, I have contributed to projects producing policy briefings, data-management guides, research resources and introductory time-use training. I have also developed and shared time-use data processing syntax on GitHub to support reproducible research.

My international research network includes collaborations and research visits with colleagues at institutions including the University of Western Australia. During my research visit to UWA, I presented my work in the Business School seminar series and collaborated on the use of time-use diary data in transport studies.

I have presented my research at more than ten national and international conferences, including the International Conference on Social Science Methodology (RC33) and the International Association for Time Use Research (IATUR) Conference.

04 · Teaching

Teaching &
Outreach

I am passionate about teaching, research methods and widening access to research.

I have more than five years of university-level teaching experience, including small-group teaching, module leadership, assessment, dissertation supervision, marking and student mentoring.

My teaching experience spans Sociology of Health, Quantitative Sociology, Research Methods and Research Design. I have also completed the Fellowship of the Higher Education Academy (FHEA).

My interest in educational outreach has led me to work with the UK Brilliant Club, where I delivered six research-based classes to high-school students in early 2025.

I have also independently designed and delivered an introductory time-use research training workshop for academics and policymakers.
