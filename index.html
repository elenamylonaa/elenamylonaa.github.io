01 · About

Researcher with
a methodological lens.

My academic background lies in Quantitative Sociology and Public Health, with a particular interest in research methods and time-use research.

I completed an ESRC 1+3 fully funded doctoral studentship at the University of Warwick, including an integrated Master's in Social Science Research Methods and a PhD in Sociology.

During my doctoral studies, I received several prestigious awards, including an ESRC Overseas Institutional Visit award and an ESRC academic events award to co-organise the Quantitative Methods Doctoral Conference at Warwick, which brought together 20 early-career researchers.

Following my doctorate, I was awarded a one-year, part-time postdoctoral fellowship at the Institute of Advanced Study, University of Warwick. This provided opportunities for interdisciplinary research, engagement with early-career researchers and innovative dissemination, alongside my continuing work at the Centre for Time Use Research at UCL.

02 · Research

Time.
Methods.
Society.

My work combines methodological development with substantive questions in sociology and public health, with a particular focus on time-use research.

I am interested in how people organise their lives, how time-use data can illuminate social questions, and how methodological innovation can strengthen quantitative social research.

Collaboration & networks

My research has involved collaboration across disciplines and institutions. During my time at the Institute of Advanced Study, I engaged with interdisciplinary early-career researchers while continuing my work at CTUR, UCL.

I was also awarded an IAS Award supporting a research visit to the University of Western Australia. There, I presented my research in the UWA Business School seminar series and collaborated on the use of time-use diary data in transport studies.

I have presented my work at more than ten national and international conferences, including the International Conference on Social Science Methodology (RC33) and the International Association for Time Use Research (IATUR) Conference.

At IATUR, I connected with Professor Jonathan Gershuny, Director of the Centre for Time Use Research at UCL, which led to a part-time position specialising in time-use research methods, a role I continue to hold.

Research in practice

At CTUR, I have led projects producing policy briefings for the UK Social Mobility Commission and the What Works Centre for Wellbeing, data-management guides for the Click and Drag Diary Instrument and the Millennium Cohort Study, and introductory time-use training workshops.

During my time at What Works for Wellbeing, I developed and shared time-use data-processing syntax on GitHub to support researchers in replicating analyses and extending existing research.
