About the researcher

Dr Elena Mylona

Sociologist · Quantitative Researcher · Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow

Dr Elena Mylona is a Sociologist and Quantitative researcher specialising in time-use research, research methods and gender inequality. Her work combines methodological innovation with substantive questions about how people organise their everyday lives.

She is currently a Marie Skłodowska-Curie Postdoctoral Fellow at the University of Cyprus, with Dr Miranda Christou as her academic advisor, while she remains a Research Associate at the Centre for Time Use Research at UCL. She holds a PhD in Sociology and an integrated Master's in Social Science Research Methods from the University of Warwick.

MUMTIME brings together these interests to investigate how working mothers in Cyprus spend and experience their time, using a sequential mixed-methods approach combining digital time diaries and qualitative interviews.

01 · The project

Who gets
time?

MUMTIME explores how working mothers in Cyprus spend, experience and negotiate their time.

The project examines the relationship between employment, motherhood and everyday time use, asking how the organisation of time shapes experiences of work–life balance, time poverty and gender inequality.

By combining detailed time-use diaries with qualitative interviews, MUMTIME brings together quantitative patterns and mothers' lived experiences.

The central question

How do mothers in Cyprus experience and organise their everyday time?

02 · Research design

From time
to experience.

A sequential mixed-methods design connects patterns in time use with the experiences behind them.

01
Time diaries
Working mothers record how they spend a 24-hour period, creating detailed data on everyday activities and time allocation.
02
Quantitative analysis
Time-use data are used to examine patterns, inequalities and experiences of time poverty and work–life balance.
03
Qualitative interviews
Interviews explore how mothers understand, negotiate and experience the demands placed on their time.
03 · Innovation

Measuring
everyday life.

MUMTIME combines methodological innovation with substantive research on gender inequality.

Digital diaries

A digital time diary approach adapted to the Cypriot context provides a detailed picture of how time is organised across the day.

Time & inequality

The project examines how paid work, domestic labour, childcare and leisure are distributed across everyday life.

Mixed methods

Quantitative analysis identifies patterns in time use, while interviews explore the meanings and experiences behind those patterns.

Open research

The project aims to contribute reusable, transparent and accessible resources for time-use research.

04 · Outputs

From data
to impact.

MUMTIME will produce both research outputs and resources for future time-use research in Cyprus.

Publications

Peer-reviewed research on motherhood, time, inequality and time-use methodology.

Data

A new source of time-use evidence focused on working mothers in Cyprus.

Research tools

An open-source digital time diary adapted to the Cypriot context.

Policy

Evidence to support discussions around gender equality, work–life balance and time poverty.
